## Soil Classes

There are 12 main classes of soils per the USDA’s NRCS division:

- Alfisols - aluminium and iron-rich clay soils which generate as ground cover in hardwood forests.    
- Andisols - extremely fertile but rare soils enriched in volcanic ash. Andisols are not bound by climate. 
- Aridisols - water and organic matter-poor soils which form the bulk of the ground cover in deserts and xeric shrublands.
- Entisols - poorly developed soils that take strongly from their parent material. These include all alluvial soils.
- Gelisols - low-fertility soils in boreal to polar regions which develop permafrost.
- Histosols - carbon-rich soils found in permanent wetlands. Histosols include peats, and are common producers of coal.
- Inceptisols - further developed entisols, also commonly found in riparian deposition zones and Mediterranean regions.
- Mollisols - deep, organic-rich soils which make up the largest grassland regions in the world, including the Great Plains and Great Steppe.
- Oxisols - clay-rich red soils found in tropical equatorial forests such as the Amazon and Congo rainforests.
- Spodosols - also known as podzol, these are the most common soils in boreal coniferous forests and heathlands.
- Ultisols - red clay soils which dominate temperate, humid environments. They are strong tree growers but poor for agriculture.
- Vertisols - shrink-swell soils rich in expansive clay minerals which form in areas with strongly defined wet and dry seasons, such as the Deccan Plateau.
