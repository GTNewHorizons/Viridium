## Forests

Forest subregions are one of the most common types of terrain within ecoregions. They exist as a modifier over the initial type of terrain, and there are several kinds of forests:

- **Tropical Rainforests** are evergreen and dense with multiple layers of flora. An emergent layer stands out above the canopy layer, where most large trees set their heights at. An understory layer of small trees and bushes exists below that. The forest floor is sparser, with no grasses growing, but ferns and flowers are common. Lianas often branch from tree-to-tree.
	- **Cloud Forests** are a subtype of tropical rainforest found in superhumid low montane regions. Cloud forests have generally lower canopies and slightly lower biodiversity, with greater amounts of ferns, tree ferns, and lianas growing. The substrates of cloud forests are also dominated by mosses. Cloud forests also have a permanent shroud of fog encompassing them. Cloud forests are most common in the Andes and in Indonesia.
- **Woodlands** are low-density, low-biodiversity forests consisting of a relatively equal distribution of large and small trees. Woodlands are transitional biomes from shrublands or savannahs, and have understories of bushes and grass to support them. They can exist in these conditions in any climate.
- **Taigas** are dense, low-biodiversity gymnosperm-dominated forests common in boreal and subpolar regions, dominated by spruce (Picea), Pine (Pinus), and Larch (Larix) trees, though stands of other trees, including the rare angiosperm, occur as well.
- **Family Dominant Forests** are forests where the dominant canopy and canopy emergent, or tallest trees, are made of at least 90% of trees of one family.
	- **Dipterocarp Jungles** are Indonesian and Indochinese forests where the dominant family is Dipterocarpaceae
	- **Palm Forests** are found in the Amazon and Caribbean, and the dominant family is Arecaceae.

## Wetlands

- **Peatlands:** Sphagnum-dominated peatlands become the dominant wetland in all superhumid tropical and boreal wetlands, 70% of wetlands in wet-humid tropical and boreal wetlands, and 50% of wet+ subtropical, temperate, and subarctic wetlands.
